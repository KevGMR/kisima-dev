  - question: "What is volumetric weight?"
    answer: "Volumetric weight is a calculation based on how much space a package occupies. For air freight, a large lightweight package can have a higher chargeable weight than its actual scale weight."
    explanation: "Air freight is charged on chargeable weight — the higher of actual weight and volumetric weight. This reflects that cargo aircraft bill for space as well as weight. Volumetric weight is calculated as: length × width × height ÷ the route-specific divisor."
  - question: "How is volumetric weight calculated?"
    answer: "Multiply length × width × height (in cm), divide by the route-specific volumetric divisor. The result is the volumetric weight in kilograms."
    bullets:
      - "Volumetric weight = (L × W × H) ÷ divisor"
      - "The divisor is route-specific, not universal"
      - "Package dimensions are in centimetres"
      - "Quantity multiplies the result when shipping multiple identical packages"
  - question: "Example: a 40×30×30cm parcel at 5kg actual weight"
    answer: "Assume a divisor of 5000. Volume is 36,000cm³. Volumetric weight = 36,000 ÷ 5000 = 7.2kg. Since 7.2kg is higher than 5kg actual, the chargeable weight is 7.2kg."
    bullets:
      - "Actual weight: 5kg"
      - "Dimensions: 40 × 30 × 30 cm"
      - "Volume: 36,000 cm³"
      - "Volumetric weight: 36,000 ÷ 5000 = 7.2kg"
      - "Chargeable weight: 7.2kg (higher than actual)"
  - question: "Does volumetric weight apply to sea freight?"
    answer: "No. Sea freight is charged on CBM (cubic metres), which is a volume measure, not a weight comparison."
  - question: "How can I reduce chargeable weight?"
    answer: "Reduce unnecessary packaging and consolidate multiple packages into fewer larger ones where practical."
    explanation: "Oversized cartons with lots of empty space inflate volumetric weight. Right-sizing packaging, removing excess filler, and combining multiple small shipments into one carton can reduce chargeable weight."
